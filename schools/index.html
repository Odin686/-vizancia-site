The claim

The Campus newspaper receives this pitch: 'An AI watering system saves 50% of water in every garden. Our two-day test proves the school should buy it.'

Would you publish that claim now? Give an initial reason and one thing you would need to know.

What can each source support?

A. Groundskeeper's test log

Campus groundskeeper • fictional local record • 4 May

Two similar beds were observed for two days. The usual schedule used 20 litres; the new system used 10. Only water use was recorded. Plant health and longer-term weather were not measured.

B. Supplier's poster

Sprout Systems • fictional advertisement • 5 May

'Save half your water everywhere!' The poster repeats the Campus test. The supplier sells the system. It gives no additional tests or methods.

C. Groundskeeper's clarification

Campus groundskeeper • fictional interview note • 6 May

'The result is promising for these beds. I have not tested a heat wave, different plants or a whole growing season. I would not promise the same savings everywhere.'

Choose a next step and explain a source, a limit and one thing to check. More than one action can be justified.

Compare the reasoning

Publish the universal claim

The figures describe two beds for two days. Repeating them in an advertisement adds no independent evidence. A universal claim goes beyond this test.

Report the small test and its limits

This is supported if you explain the conditions and missing measures. You can report 10 versus 20 litres in this test without promising universal savings or healthy plants.

Wait and request a better test

This is reasonable if the article's purpose is to recommend a purchase. Specify what you need: more conditions, longer observation, plant health and an independent check.

Facilitator note

A supports a narrow description of observed water use, not plant health or universal savings. B is a sales claim based on A, not independent corroboration. C narrows the interpretation. Accept a qualified report or a justified pause; score reasoning, not agreement with one preferred action.

Before sharing a device

Temporary Classroom Mode clears its session on end, timeout or backgrounding. Use paper to keep work. Separate saved profiles are local and are not password protected.

Passport entries are not automatically shared with a teacher or Vizancia. The pilot asks for teacher feedback and school-approved aggregates, not pupil journals or identified work.

School staff decide the approved format and supervise learners. The app preview still needs each school's device and accessibility review.
